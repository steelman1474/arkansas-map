    How local government works in Arkansas

    Counties are the main layer of local government. The county judge is the elected executive, not a judge in the usual sense. The quorum court, made up of justices of the peace, is the legislature. Both were set up by Amendment 55 in 1974.

    Cities and towns are incorporated municipalities with their own mayor and council. The Census counts places of 500 or more people as cities and smaller ones as towns. Census-designated places are unincorporated communities defined only for statistics; the county governs them directly.

    County seats are where the courthouse and county offices sit. Ten counties have two seats, mostly a holdover from when travel across the county was slow.

    Townships are Arkansas's minor civil divisions. They have no government and today mostly serve as constable election districts. County courts can merge or redraw them, which is why Benton, Calhoun, Crawford and Sebastian counties now use numbered townships.
