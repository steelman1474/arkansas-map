    How Arkansas is divided

    Counties are the main layer of local government. The county judge is the elected executive, not a judge in the usual sense. The quorum court, made up of justices of the peace, is the legislature. Both were set up by Amendment 55 (1974).

    Cities and towns are incorporated municipalities. The Census counts places of 500 or more people as cities and smaller ones as towns. CDPs are unincorporated communities that the Census defines only for statistics.

    County seats are where the courthouse and county offices sit. Ten Arkansas counties have two seats, mostly a holdover from when travel across the county was slow.

    Townships are Arkansas's minor civil divisions (MCDs). They have no government; today they mostly serve as constable election districts. County courts can merge or redraw them, which is why some counties (Benton, Calhoun, Crawford, Sebastian) now use numbered townships.
